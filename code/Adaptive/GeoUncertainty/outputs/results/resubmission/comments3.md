The revised manuscript demonstrates that the authors have thoughtfully addressed the reviewer's primary concerns regarding interpretive discipline. The mediation analysis has been appropriately reframed as a statistical decomposition rather than a causal mechanism, and the comparative claims positioning ambiguity as the primary channel relative to volatility have been appropriately moderated. These revisions significantly enhance the alignment between empirical evidence and result interpretation, directly responding to concerns about overstatement.

However, several aspects of the manuscript warrant further improvement prior to publication. First, while the language comparing ambiguity and volatility has been moderated, the narrative continues to implicitly position ambiguity as the dominant mechanism in several passages. The discussion would benefit from presenting ambiguity more explicitly as a complementary source of uncertainty alongside volatility, rather than suggesting systematic dominance over existing risk measures. Second, the newly proposed cross-entropy-based ambiguity measure represents a valuable methodological contribution, but its empirical justification could be strengthened through discussion of alternative ambiguity proxies in the literature, along with additional rationale for why the proposed measure captures a distinct dimension of uncertainty.

Third, the interpretation of economic magnitudes should be presented with greater caution. Certain statements regarding annualized effects and portfolio implications appear stronger than warranted by the empirical design and should be moderated to remain consistent with the largely associational nature of the results. Fourth, although the authors acknowledge the limitations of the mediation framework, the Baron–Kenny approach remains relatively uncommon in asset-pricing literature, and the paper would benefit from more explicit discussion of identification limitations associated with mediation analysis in financial return data. Fifth, the instrumental variable strategy based on "Non-Asian GPR" shocks requires clearer justification, particularly regarding the plausibility of the exclusion restriction and the possibility that global geopolitical shocks could influence Chinese markets through alternative contemporaneous channels.

Sixth, the timing assumptions underlying the empirical strategy merit further clarification. While the argument that geopolitical news arrives prior to the Chinese trading session and is incorporated within the same day appears reasonable, additional discussion and potential robustness checks would strengthen this claim. Seventh, the paper could more clearly situate its contribution within the broader literature on uncertainty, ambiguity aversion, and geopolitical risk in financial markets. Although relevant references are included, the discussion should more explicitly articulate how the paper extends or complements existing empirical findings. Eighth, the specification of regression models and description of control variables could be streamlined to enhance readability and transparency. Ninth, the presentation of empirical results, particularly in tabular format, could be simplified to facilitate reader comprehension of key findings and their economic interpretation. Finally, while the manuscript is generally well-written, minor grammatical and stylistic inconsistencies throughout the text should be addressed.

In summary, the authors have clearly undertaken substantial revisions and have effectively addressed the reviewer's central concern regarding interpretive overstatement. The empirical topic—geopolitical risk, ambiguity, and equity returns—falls appropriately within the scope of Finance Research Letters and aligns with the journal's focus on timely financial market issues. Overall, the manuscript appears suitable for publication in this journal, but would benefit from one additional revision cycle to address the remaining issues of clarity, interpretation, and presentation, rather than consideration for alternative publication venues.
