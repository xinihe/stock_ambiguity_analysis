> Reviewer #2: This manuscript has already undergone at least one substantive review round, and it is evident that the paper has benefited from that process. The empirical analysis is carefully executed, technically competent, and supported by an extensive set of robustness checks. In this respect, I broadly share the assessment of the previous referee and do not have additional substantive comments on the empirical set-up itself. Reiterating those issues would, in my view, not be particularly productive at this stage.
>
> From my perspective, the main remaining concern lies not in the execution of the empirical work, but in the interpretation and framing of the results. While the empirical findings are generally sound, I find that at several points the manuscript advances causal and comparative claims that extend beyond what can be cleanly identified from the data. In particular, there are instances where results that are best interpreted as consistent with a proposed mechanism are described using language that suggests the mechanism is demonstrated or established, without a corresponding increase in identification strength. Some causal interpretations therefore appear to rest on assumptions that cannot be empirically verified within the current research design.
>
> This issue is especially relevant in the mediation analysis. While mediation frameworks can be useful for organizing and summarizing empirical relationships, I am not persuaded that the estimated indirect effects can be interpreted as a causal decomposition under the present assumptions. Clarifications regarding timing, robustness checks, or alternative specifications are helpful, but in my view they do not resolve the underlying identification concern.
>
> Relatedly, I find that the manuscript frequently employs strong comparative language when positioning ambiguity relative to volatility. Statements suggesting that ambiguity represents the "primary" transmission channel, or that volatility's role is systematically overstated, seem difficult to justify given the level of identification achieved. To me, these comparisons reflect interpretive choices rather than conclusions that are compelled by the empirical evidence.
>
> Importantly, this concern is not limited to a small number of isolated passages. Similar interpretive language appears in multiple parts of the manuscript, including the Introduction, the discussion of results, and the policy implications. As a result, I am not convinced that limited or purely cosmetic edits would be sufficient to address the core issue.
>
> To be clear, I am not suggesting that additional empirical tests or further robustness exercises are necessary, nor do I view the results themselves as uninteresting or flawed. Rather, my concern is that the current narrative repeatedly goes beyond what the evidence can strictly sustain. Addressing this issue would require a fundamental shift in interpretive stance rather than further technical elaboration.
>
> In sum, from my standpoint as a referee, the paper's remaining weaknesses lie not in empirical execution, but in interpretive discipline. Given that this issue has persisted despite a prior major revision opportunity, I remain concerned about the manuscript's suitability in its current form.
